## AI Diary

This part of the session felt like the payoff of the architecture decisions
from the earlier retro. Because I'd already separated "looping SMIL
animation" from "static positional transform" as two distinct mechanisms,
adding sprint and prone was almost mechanical — new numbers in a table,
zero new plumbing. Belly-up was the one that needed real thought: my first
instinct was to wonder whether I'd need a second skeleton or new artwork for
a dog lying on its back, since the existing character parts are all
authored assuming a standing side profile. Working through the geometry —
picking a pivot near the character's own vertical center so a ~175°
rotation maps the ground-level feet up to where the head used to be, and
the head down near the ground — turned out to produce exactly "legs in the
air" without touching a single box coordinate. That it worked on the first
screenshot pass (unlike the sit/lie pivot, which needed a retake last time)
was a nice confirmation that the earlier mistake actually taught me
something: I checked the lever-arm distance from the pivot to the
farthest part of the silhouette before committing to numbers this time,
instead of just reasoning that "a hinge story sounds physically clean."

[→ AGENT DECISION] I didn't actually catch a wrong decision in this
segment — the design held up on the first try. If I'm honest, the closest
thing to a misstep was moving straight to implementation from H's Thai

## Lessons Learned

- **When a design mistake gets diagnosed and fixed (like the sit/lie pivot
  lever-arm issue), explicitly re-apply the diagnosis as a check on the
  NEXT similar decision** rather than assuming the lesson is now "baked
  in." Concretely here: before finalizing bellyup's rotation pivot, I
  deliberately checked the lever-arm distance to the silhouette's farthest
  point — the exact thing I'd skipped before sit/lie and paid for. Turning
  a retro lesson into a literal pre-commit checklist item (even a mental
  one-line check) is what makes a lesson actually change future behavior,
  versus just being a paragraph in a file nobody re-reads mid-task.
- **A short, literal translation of a user's native-language request (e.g.
  หมอบ = "crouch/prone") is not the same as a confirmed shared
  understanding of what the resulting artifact should look like.** For
  visual/creative asks where the deliverable can't be cheaply previewed to
  the user before committing to it, either show the concrete result before
  finalizing, or explicitly restate your interpretation in one sentence
  and let silence-as-approval be a deliberate, acknowledged risk — not an
  unexamined default.
- **Reusable test/debug scaffolding (a screenshot rig, a mock harness) pays
  for itself the second time you need it** — extending `pose-tour.html`
  for the new `rootFlip` transform took one small edit instead of rebuilding
  verification tooling from scratch, directly because the first retro's
  friction (headless Chrome quirks, zsh word-splitting) had already been
  paid down and the resulting rig was kept around.
